# Responsive Design

Responsive design changes composition and interaction; it is not merely shrinking desktop content.

Identify minimum viewport, content priority, and what stacks, collapses, scrolls, moves, or becomes contextual. Preserve task priority. Test long labels, translated content, large text, and orientation where relevant.

Common adaptations: sidebars become drawers/compact navigation; dense tables get horizontal scroll, priority columns, or card alternatives; toolbars wrap or move secondary actions to overflow; dialogs may become full-screen sheets.

Avoid fixed-width overflow, tiny mobile text, hover-only essential actions, unexplained gestures, and breakpoints chosen only by device brand.

Verify narrow mobile, typical mobile, intermediate/tablet, desktop, and wide desktop where relevant. Record tested sizes.
